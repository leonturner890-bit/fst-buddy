# Agile & Scrum

## Agile as a mindset, not a process
The Agile Manifesto (2001) values individuals and interactions over
processes and tools, working software over comprehensive documentation,
customer collaboration over contract negotiation, and responding to
change over following a plan. Agile itself isn't a methodology you can
"do" step by step — Scrum, Kanban, XP, and others are concrete
methodologies built on Agile values. A student saying "we're doing Agile"
without naming a framework is describing a philosophy, not a process.

## Scrum roles
- **Product Owner** — owns the product backlog and decides *what* gets
  built and in what priority order, representing stakeholder/customer
  interests.
- **Scrum Master** — a facilitator who protects the team's process, removes
  blockers, and runs ceremonies; not a project manager who assigns tasks.
- **Development Team** — the people building the product; self-organizing
  around *how* to deliver the sprint goal.

## Scrum events
- **Sprint** — a fixed-length iteration (commonly 1–4 weeks) that produces
  a potentially shippable increment.
- **Sprint Planning** — the team pulls items from the backlog into the
  sprint and commits to a sprint goal.
- **Daily Scrum ("standup")** — a short daily sync on progress and
  blockers; not a status report to a manager.
- **Sprint Review** — demo the increment to stakeholders and gather
  feedback.
- **Sprint Retrospective** — the team reflects on *how* they worked and
  agrees on process improvements for next sprint.

## Kanban vs. Scrum
Kanban has no fixed-length iterations — work flows continuously through
columns (e.g. To Do / In Progress / Done) with a limit on how much can be
"in progress" at once (WIP limits), and there are no prescribed roles or
ceremonies. Scrum is time-boxed and role-based; Kanban is continuous-flow
and less prescriptive.

## Common misconceptions worth correcting
- "A sprint backlog and a product backlog are the same thing." The
  product backlog is the full, prioritized list of everything that might
  ever be built; the sprint backlog is the subset pulled into the
  *current* sprint.
- "Story points measure time/hours." They measure relative effort/
  complexity/uncertainty, deliberately decoupled from calendar time so
  estimates don't get treated as promises.
- "The Scrum Master assigns tasks to developers." The team self-organizes
  around who does what; the Scrum Master's job is process facilitation
  and removing obstacles, not task assignment.
